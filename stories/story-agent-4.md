# The Cartographer of Small Thunder

Mara kept a map of every sound the town had ever lost.

The church bell that cracked in winter was a blue spiral. Her brother’s laugh, gone to the capital and never posted back, was a bright road that ended at the edge of the paper. Even the old millwheel, silent since the river changed its mind, had a brown circle beside the reeds.

People brought her silences in jars, boxes, folded handkerchiefs. Mara listened, dipped her pen, and marked where each absence belonged.

One evening a child arrived with empty hands.

“I lost tomorrow’s thunder,” he said.

Mara frowned. “Thunder hasn’t happened yet.”

“It was supposed to,” he whispered. “Now the clouds are afraid.”

So Mara climbed the hill and unrolled her map beneath a sky packed with dark, trembling sheep. She found a blank space over the child’s house, drew the smallest jagged line she could, and wrote: courage, returning.

The clouds read it.

A soft boom crossed the valley—not loud enough to frighten anyone, just enough to remind the rain what it was for.

By morning, every roof was shining.
