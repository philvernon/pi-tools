# The Clockmaker's Sparrow

Every morning, Mira wound the tower clock and found a brass sparrow waiting on the sill.

It was not there when she climbed the stairs. It was not there when she unlocked the gear room. But after the last turn of the key, when the pendulum gathered its courage and the bells leaned toward the hour, the little bird appeared with its head cocked, listening.

Mira had built hundreds of mechanical things, but never anything that arrived by itself.

On the winter solstice, the clock refused to strike noon. The town below held its breath. Bread stopped rising, horses stopped stamping, and snow hung in the air as if pinned to blue cloth.

The sparrow tapped the glass with one wing.

Mira opened the window. The bird hopped inside, crossed the workbench, and pecked at a forgotten drawer. In it lay her father's final gear, unfinished, its teeth marked in his careful hand. Mira filed it smooth, set it into the heart of the clock, and stepped back.

The tower rang once.

Snow fell. Bread rose. Horses remembered their feet.

The sparrow flew into the open clockwork and became, with a click no louder than a seed dropping, the smallest moving part of all.

After that, Mira still wound the clock each morning. And each morning, just before the hour, she heard wings inside the gears.
