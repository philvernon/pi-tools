# The Lantern Orchard

Mira found the orchard after sunset, when the road behind her had folded itself into mist.

Every tree held a lantern instead of fruit: blue glass, green glass, amber glass, each one glowing with a different remembered summer. She reached for a small red lantern and heard her brother laughing on the riverbank, years before the fever took him.

A keeper stepped from between the trunks. “You may take one,” he said, “but only one. Carry it too long, and you’ll forget how to live outside its light.”

Mira held the red lantern until dawn silvered the leaves. Then she hung it back on its branch.

“I thought you came for what you lost,” said the keeper.

“I did,” Mira answered, wiping her eyes. “And now I know where to visit.”

She walked home by morning, empty-handed except for the road ahead.
