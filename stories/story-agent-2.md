# The Lighthouse That Remembered Rain

Every evening, Mara climbed the spiral stairs of the abandoned lighthouse and polished the lamp that no longer lit. Ships had stopped using the harbor years ago, but she liked the ritual: cloth, brass, glass, silence.

One drought summer, when the wells coughed dust and the gulls flew inland, Mara found a drop of water trembling inside the lamp. It rolled across the glass against gravity, bright as a star. When she touched it, the lighthouse filled with the sound of rain—old rain, remembered rain, storms stored in stone.

Mara listened. Then she opened every window.

Clouds gathered from the sea like animals returning home. By dawn, rain stitched the village back together: barrels sang, roofs drummed, children ran barefoot through silver streets.

Afterward, people asked Mara what miracle she had performed.

She only shrugged and kept polishing the lamp.

Some things, she knew, shine best when they are remembered.
