# The Cartographer of Small Silences

Mira made maps of places no one thought to chart: the pause before a kettle sang, the hush beneath a sleeping dog's ear, the tiny quiet that settled over the city when every traffic light turned red at once.

Her atlas was a stack of blue notebooks tied with thread. People laughed when she opened them at the market, but they still leaned close.

"Where do I find courage?" asked the baker, flour on his sleeves.

Mira turned to a page marked with crumbs. "Between the second knock and the door opening. Stand there long enough."

"Where is forgiveness?" whispered a woman holding an unopened letter.

Mira traced a finger along a blank margin. "Here. After the last excuse, before the first honest word."

One winter morning, Mira discovered a silence she had never mapped. It waited in her own room after the last notebook was filled. No kettle, no dog, no city breath. Just the question of what to do when every quiet place had been named.

She sat with it until dusk. Then she wrote on the cover of a new notebook: *The silence after finishing is where beginnings hide.*

By spring, people stopped laughing. They brought her empty books instead.
